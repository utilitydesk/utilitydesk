What Is In-Hand Salary?

In-hand salary, also known as take-home salary or net salary, is the actual amount you receive in your bank account every month after all deductions are made from your CTC (Cost to Company). Every salaried professional in India should understand the difference between their CTC and their in-hand salary, because the gap between the two can be significant — often 15-30% of the CTC is deducted before the money reaches your account.

CTC includes components like employer PF contribution, gratuity, health insurance premiums, and other perks that you never see in your bank account. Your in-hand salary is computed by deducting statutory obligations such as Employee Provident Fund (EPF), Professional Tax (PT), Employee State Insurance (ESI), and Income Tax (TDS) from your gross salary. Understanding these deductions helps you plan your monthly budget accurately, negotiate better salary structures, and optimise your tax liabilities.

This calculator simplifies the entire process. Enter your annual CTC, specify the basic salary percentage, and the tool computes your monthly take-home pay with a detailed breakdown of every deduction. Whether you are switching jobs, evaluating an offer letter, or simply auditing your monthly finances, knowing your precise in-hand salary empowers you to make smarter financial decisions.

Components of a Typical Salary Structure

A typical salary structure in India includes the following components:

  • Basic Salary: The foundation of your salary, usually 40-50% of CTC. All statutory deductions like PF and gratuity are calculated based on this.
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA): Provided to meet rental accommodation expenses. Can be partially or fully exempt from tax under the old regime.
  • Special Allowance: A flexible component that can be structured to optimise tax benefits or simply paid as part of take-home.
  • Dearness Allowance (DA): Provided to cope with inflation, usually a percentage of basic salary.
  • Leave Travel Allowance (LTA): Tax-exempt allowance for travel expenses during leave, available under the old tax regime.

Formula Used

The in-hand salary is computed in two stages. First, your gross monthly salary is derived from the annual CTC. Then, all mandatory and voluntary deductions are subtracted to arrive at the final take-home amount.

Formula
Gross Salary = CTC ÷ 12 In-Hand Salary = Gross − (PF + PT + ESI + TDS + Other Deductions)

Where:

  • PF = Employee Provident Fund contribution (12% of basic salary per month)
  • PT = Professional Tax (varies by state; maximum ₹2,500 per year)
  • ESI = Employee State Insurance (0.75% of gross, applicable only if gross ≤ ₹21,000/month)
  • TDS = Tax Deducted at Source (based on applicable income tax slab)
  • Other Deductions = Insurance premiums, loan recoveries, union dues, etc.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many professionals make these errors when estimating their in-hand salary. Being aware of them helps you plan your finances more accurately.

✕

Comparing Only CTC Across Offers

Two offers with the same CTC can yield very different in-hand salaries depending on the salary structure, basic percentage, and variable pay split. Always compare take-home pay, not just CTC.

✕

Ignoring Variable Pay Component

Variable pay is often 10-20% of CTC but is not paid monthly. If you include it when budgeting your monthly expenses, you will overestimate your disposable income and may face cash-flow problems.

✕

Not Accounting for Professional Tax

Professional tax varies by state and can be up to ₹2,500 per year. Many people overlook this deduction, especially when moving between states, and are surprised by the reduced take-home pay.

✕

Assuming Employer PF Is Part of Take-Home

Employer PF contribution (12% of basic) is part of your CTC but never reaches your bank account. It goes directly into your EPF account. Treat it as retirement savings, not monthly income.

✕

Forgetting Gratuity Accrual

Gratuity (4.81% of basic) is a non-cash component included in CTC. You only receive it when you leave the company after 5 years of service. It should not be counted as monthly income.

✕

Not Choosing the Right Tax Regime

Sticking with the old tax regime when you have few deductions, or vice versa, can reduce your in-hand salary by thousands per month. Compare both regimes every year before the financial year begins.

Latest Government Rules — FY 2025-26

Stay updated with the most recent changes to salary deductions, tax slabs, and statutory limits that directly impact your in-hand salary.

FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27

Key Updates for Salaried Employees

  • Standard deduction under the new tax regime has been increased to ₹75,000 per year (from ₹50,000 previously). Under the old regime, the standard deduction remains at ₹50,000.
  • ESI wage ceiling remains at ₹21,000 per month. Employees drawing gross wages up to this limit are covered under the ESI scheme with employee contribution at 0.75% and employer contribution at 3.25%.
  • Professional tax continues to vary by state. Maharashtra levies ₹2,500/year (₹200/month for 11 months + ₹300 in February), Karnataka charges ₹2,500/year, West Bengal ₹2,500/year, and Telangana ₹2,500/year. Several states including Delhi, Rajasthan, and UP do not levy PT.
  • Employee PF contribution remains 12% of basic salary + DA. The wage ceiling for mandatory PF enrolment is ₹15,000/month basic, but employees earning above this can also voluntarily join.
  • New tax regime slabs for FY 2025-26: Nil up to ₹3L, 5% for ₹3-7L, 10% for ₹7-10L, 15% for ₹10-12L, 20% for ₹12-15L, 30% above ₹15L. Section 87A rebate provides full tax exemption for taxable income up to ₹7L under the new regime.
  • Employer NPS contribution deduction has been increased from 10% to 14% of basic salary for private sector employees under the old tax regime.

How to Increase Your In-Hand Salary

While you cannot avoid mandatory deductions, you can optimise your salary structure to increase your in-hand salary:

  • Optimise Tax Regime: Choose between old and new tax regime based on your deductions to minimise TDS. Use our Income Tax Calculator to compare both.
  • Structure HRA Wisely: If you pay rent, claim HRA exemption under the old regime to reduce taxable income and increase take-home pay.
  • Use Tax-Saving Investments: Invest in Section 80C instruments (EPF, PPF, ELSS, LIC) to reduce taxable income under the old regime by up to ₹1,50,000.
  • Negotiate Salary Components: During salary negotiations, focus on components that increase take-home pay rather than non-cash benefits like excessive gratuity or deferred compensation.
  • Claim Standard Deduction: Ensure your employer factors in the ₹75,000 standard deduction (new regime) to reduce monthly TDS and boost your in-hand salary.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about in-hand salary, deductions, and take-home pay

What is in-hand salary?
In-hand salary, also known as take-home salary or net salary, is the actual amount you receive in your bank account after all deductions like PF, professional tax, income tax (TDS), ESI, and other allowances are subtracted from your gross salary or CTC.
How is in-hand salary calculated?
In-hand salary is calculated using the formula: In-Hand Salary = Gross Salary − (Employee PF + Professional Tax + ESI + TDS + Other Deductions). Gross salary is your CTC divided by 12, minus any non-cash components like gratuity or employer PF.
What deductions are made from salary?
Common salary deductions include: Employee Provident Fund (12% of basic), Professional Tax (varies by state, max ₹2,500/year), Income Tax/TDS (based on income tax slabs), ESI (0.75% if gross below ₹21,000), and other deductions like insurance premiums, loan recoveries, or company-specific deductions.
What is the difference between CTC and in-hand salary?
CTC (Cost to Company) is the total cost your employer spends on you, including employer PF, gratuity, insurance, and other benefits. In-hand salary is what you actually receive after all deductions. Typically, in-hand salary is 70-85% of CTC depending on the salary structure and deductions.
How much PF is deducted from salary?
Employee PF contribution is 12% of basic salary plus dearness allowance (if any). The employer also contributes 12%, but 8.33% goes to EPS (Employee Pension Scheme) and 3.67% to EPF. Only the employee's 12% contribution is deducted from your in-hand salary.
What is professional tax and who pays it?
Professional tax is a state-level tax levied on salaried individuals and professionals. It varies by state and is deducted monthly by the employer. The maximum professional tax in India is ₹2,500 per year. States like Maharashtra, Karnataka, West Bengal, and Telangana levy professional tax, while states like Delhi and Rajasthan do not.
What is ESI deduction and who is eligible?
ESI (Employee State Insurance) is a social security scheme for employees earning gross salary up to ₹21,000 per month. The employee contributes 0.75% of gross salary and the employer contributes 3.25%. ESI provides medical benefits, sickness benefits, and maternity benefits to covered employees.
How can I increase my in-hand salary?
You can optimise your in-hand salary by choosing the right tax regime (old vs new), structuring HRA wisely if you pay rent, investing in tax-saving instruments under Section 80C, and negotiating salary components that increase take-home pay. Avoiding unnecessary voluntary deductions also helps.
Is gross salary same as in-hand salary?
No, gross salary is not the same as in-hand salary. Gross salary is your salary before statutory deductions like PF, PT, TDS, and ESI. In-hand salary is what remains after all these mandatory and voluntary deductions are subtracted from your gross salary.
What is the standard deduction for FY 2025-26?
For FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), the standard deduction for salaried employees under the new tax regime has been increased to ₹75,000. Under the old tax regime, the standard deduction remains at ₹50,000. This deduction is available directly from your gross salary before computing taxable income.
Does variable pay affect in-hand salary?
Yes, variable pay can significantly affect your in-hand salary. Variable pay is typically not paid monthly and may be paid quarterly or annually. When calculating your expected monthly in-hand salary, you should exclude variable pay from your CTC to avoid overestimating your take-home pay.
How does the new tax regime affect in-hand salary?
The new tax regime offers lower tax rates but limits most deductions and exemptions. For FY 2025-26, the new regime slabs are more favourable with a standard deduction of ₹75,000. If you have few deductions like 80C investments or HRA, the new regime may increase your in-hand salary due to lower TDS.
