What Is an EMI Calculator?

An EMI Calculator is a free online financial tool that instantly computes your Equated Monthly Installment for any type of loan ó including home loans, car loans, personal loans, education loans, and business loans. It breaks down your monthly payment into principal and interest components, shows the total interest payable over the loan tenure, and displays the total amount you will repay.

Whether you are planning to buy your first home, finance a new car, or take a personal loan for an emergency, understanding your EMI is the first step toward responsible borrowing. Our calculator uses the standard reducing-balance formula mandated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI), giving you accurate results that match what banks and NBFCs quote.

Using an EMI calculator before applying for a loan helps you compare offers from different lenders, choose the right tenure, plan your monthly budget, and avoid over-borrowing. It also lets you model prepayment scenarios to see how extra payments can save you lakhs in interest.

EMI Formula Used

All banks and financial institutions in India use the following standard formula to compute your monthly EMI on a reducing-balance loan:

EMI = P ◊ r ◊ (1 + r)n / [(1 + r)n ‚àí 1]
P = Principal (loan amount) r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ˜ 12 ˜ 100) n = Total number of months (tenure in years ◊ 12)

The formula accounts for the fact that interest is charged only on the outstanding principal balance, which decreases with every EMI you pay. This means your interest component shrinks over time while the principal component grows ó even though the total EMI stays the same throughout the tenure.

Step 2: Convert to monthly values

n = 20 ◊ 12 = 240 months

Step 3: Apply the formula

This means you will pay back more than double the original loan amount over 20 years. That is why even a small reduction in interest rate or a strategic prepayment plan can save you lakhs.

Latest Government Rules & Regulations (FY 2025-26)

Stay informed about the current regulatory framework governing loans and EMIs in India:

RBI Repo Rate & Monetary Policy

  • Current Repo Rate: The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) sets the repo rate, which directly influences lending rates across banks. As of FY 2025-26, borrowers with floating-rate loans see EMI changes linked to repo rate revisions.
  • External Benchmark Lending Rate (EBLR): Since October 2019, all new floating-rate loans for individuals must be linked to an external benchmark (usually the repo rate). Lenders must transmit rate changes within one month.

Home Loan Tax Benefits (FY 2025-26)

  • Section 80C: Deduction up to ?1.5 lakh on principal repayment of home loan (available under old tax regime).
  • Section 24(b): Deduction up to ?2 lakh per year on home loan interest for self-occupied property.
  • Section 80EE: Additional ?50,000 deduction on home loan interest for first-time homebuyers (loan up to ?35 lakh, property value up to ?50 lakh).
  • Joint Home Loans: Each co-borrower can claim separate deductions under 80C and 24(b) based on their share of the loan.

RBI Fair Practices Code for Borrowers

  • Zero Prepayment Penalty: Floating-rate term loans for individuals (home, car, personal) carry zero prepayment charges as per RBI guidelines.
  • Transparency in Interest Rates: Lenders must display the Annual Percentage Rate (APR) that includes all charges, making it easier to compare loan offers.
  • Grace Period & Penal Charges: Lenders can levy penal interest (not penalty charges) for missed EMIs, but the rate must be reasonable and disclosed upfront in the loan agreement.
  • Loan Account Statement: Banks must provide a statement at the start of each year showing principal repaid, interest paid, and outstanding balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about EMI, loans, and repayment

What is EMI?
EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is a fixed payment amount made by a borrower to a lender at a specified date each calendar month. EMI consists of both principal and interest components, and the amount remains constant throughout the loan tenure.
How is EMI calculated?
EMI is calculated using the formula: EMI = P ◊ r ◊ (1 + r)^n / [(1 + r)^n - 1], where P is the principal loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate/12/100), and n is the loan tenure in months. The EMI remains constant, but the proportion of principal and interest changes each month.
What is the difference between reducing balance and flat rate?
In reducing balance method, interest is calculated on the outstanding principal amount, which reduces with each EMI payment. In flat rate method, interest is calculated on the original principal amount throughout the loan tenure. Reducing balance is more beneficial for borrowers as the total interest paid is lower. Most banks in India now use the reducing balance method.
How can I reduce my EMI?
You can reduce your EMI by: 1) Increasing the loan tenure (though this increases total interest), 2) Making a larger down payment to reduce the principal amount, 3) Negotiating a lower interest rate with the lender, 4) Making prepayments or part-payments to reduce the principal outstanding.
What is a good EMI to income ratio?
Financial experts recommend that your total EMI obligations should not exceed 40-50% of your monthly income. For example, if your monthly income is ?50,000, your total EMI (including all loans) should not exceed ?20,000-25,000. This ensures you have enough income for other expenses and savings.
What is the difference between fixed and floating interest rate?
A fixed interest rate remains constant throughout the loan tenure, giving you predictable EMIs. A floating (or variable) interest rate changes with market conditions and the lender's base rate, meaning your EMI can go up or down. Fixed rates offer stability while floating rates can be cheaper when rates are falling. Most home loans in India are floating-rate by default.
Can I prepay my loan to reduce EMI?
Yes, you can prepay your loan partially or fully. Partial prepayment reduces the outstanding principal, and you can choose to either reduce your EMI or shorten the tenure. Under RBI's fair practices code, most floating rate loans have zero prepayment penalties. Always check your loan agreement for specific terms before making prepayments.
What happens if I miss an EMI payment?
Missing an EMI payment leads to late payment charges, negatively impacts your credit score, and may result in penal interest. Repeated defaults can lead to loan classification as NPA (Non-Performing Asset). Most lenders offer a grace period of 2-3 days, but it's crucial to communicate with your lender if you anticipate payment difficulties.
What is a moratorium period in loans?
A moratorium period is a temporary suspension of EMI payments allowed by the lender. During this period, interest continues to accrue on the outstanding principal. Education loans typically have a moratorium during the course of study plus one year after employment. Some lenders offer moratorium on other loans during financial hardships, but the accrued interest gets added to your principal.
How does loan tenure affect total interest?
Longer loan tenure reduces your monthly EMI but significantly increases the total interest paid over the life of the loan. For example, a ?50 lakh home loan at 8.5% for 20 years costs ?54.14 lakh in total interest, while the same loan for 15 years costs ?43.08 lakh. Shorter tenure saves interest but requires higher monthly payments. Choose a tenure that balances affordability with total cost.
Is EMI the same as interest rate?
No. EMI is the total monthly payment that includes both principal and interest components. The interest rate is the percentage charged by the lender on the outstanding principal. Your EMI is determined by the principal amount, interest rate, and loan tenure combined. Two loans with the same interest rate can have very different EMIs based on the amount and duration.
What is the effective interest rate on a loan?
The effective interest rate (EIR) is the actual cost of borrowing when you factor in processing fees, insurance, and other charges along with the nominal interest rate. A loan advertised at 8.5% with high processing fees may effectively cost 9.2% or more. Always compare EIR, not just nominal rates, when choosing a lender. The RBI mandates that lenders display the APR to help borrowers compare accurately.
What tax benefits are available on loan EMIs?
Under the Income Tax Act, home loan interest is deductible up to ?2 lakh per year under Section 24(b), and principal repayment is deductible up to ?1.5 lakh under Section 80C. First-time homebuyers can claim an additional ?50,000 deduction under Section 80EE. Personal and car loan interest generally do not qualify for tax deductions unless used for specific purposes like business or home renovation. Consult a tax advisor for your specific situation.
