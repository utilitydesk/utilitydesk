    Frequently Asked Questions

    What is Professional Tax?
    Professional Tax (PT) is a tax levied by state governments in India on individuals earning income through employment, trade, or profession. It is governed by Article 276 of the Constitution of India. The tax is deducted monthly from salary by the employer and deposited with the state government. The maximum amount any state can levy is ₹2,500 per year.
    Which states levy Professional Tax in India?
    The following states and union territories levy Professional Tax: Karnataka, Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, Odisha, Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Tripura, Sikkim, Meghalaya, Mizoram, and Greater Hyderabad Municipal Corporation. States like Delhi, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, and Uttarakhand do not levy PT.
    How is Professional Tax calculated?
    Professional Tax is calculated based on state-specific slab rates. Each state defines salary brackets and corresponding PT amounts. For salaried employees, the employer deducts PT monthly based on gross salary. For self-employed individuals, it is typically paid annually or half-yearly as per state rules. Your gross monthly salary determines which slab you fall into.
    Is Professional Tax deductible from income tax?
    Yes, Professional Tax is deductible under Section 16(iii) of the Income Tax Act, 1961. The PT amount paid during the financial year can be claimed as a deduction from salary income while computing taxable income. This deduction is available under both old and new tax regimes. Ensure your Form 16 reflects the PT deducted.
    What is the maximum Professional Tax?
    The maximum Professional Tax that can be levied by any state government is ₹2,500 per annum per individual, as prescribed under Article 276(2) of the Constitution of India. States like Maharashtra levy the full ₹2,500, while other states may levy lesser amounts. The cap ensures uniformity across the country despite varying slab structures.
    Who pays Professional Tax?
    Professional Tax is paid by all salaried employees, professionals (doctors, lawyers, CAs, consultants), traders, and self-employed individuals earning income in states that levy PT. Employers are responsible for deducting PT from employees' salaries and depositing it with the state government. Self-employed individuals must register and pay PT directly through the state's commercial tax department.
    How to pay Professional Tax?
    For salaried employees, PT is auto-deducted by the employer from monthly salary and deposited with the state government — no action needed from the employee. Self-employed individuals can pay PT online through the respective state's commercial tax portal, at designated banks, or at the local PT collection offices. Payment frequency varies by state: monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, or annually.
    Is Professional Tax same in all states?
    No, Professional Tax rates and slabs differ significantly across states. Each state government sets its own slab structure and rates. For example, Maharashtra has slabs starting from ₹7,500 while Karnataka starts from ₹15,000. Some states like Delhi, Haryana, Punjab, and Uttar Pradesh do not levy PT at all. Always check your specific state's slab structure for accurate calculation.
    What is a PT slab?
    A PT slab defines a salary range and the corresponding Professional Tax amount applicable. States create multiple slabs with increasing salary thresholds and tax amounts. Your gross monthly salary determines which slab you fall into and how much PT you must pay. For example, in Maharashtra, a monthly salary between ₹10,001 and ₹25,000 attracts ₹200 PT, while above ₹25,000 attracts ₹300.
    Can I claim exemption from Professional Tax?
    Certain categories may be exempt from Professional Tax depending on state rules. Common exemptions include: armed forces personnel, parents/guardians of mentally or physically disabled persons (in some states), individuals above 65 years of age (in some states), and women in certain states. Exemptions vary by state — check your state's specific exemption rules or consult a tax professional.

    How Professional Tax is Calculated

    Professional Tax in India is a state-level tax calculated using slab-based rates. Each state defines salary brackets and corresponding PT amounts. The calculation is straightforward — identify your slab based on gross monthly salary, and the corresponding PT amount is your liability.

    Monthly PT = Slab amount for your gross salary bracket Annual PT = Monthly PT × 12 (capped at ₹2,500 as per Constitution Article 276)

    Key Rules

    • Each state defines its own slab structure and PT rates
    • Maximum PT across all states is capped at ₹2,500 per year
    • Employers deduct PT monthly for salaried employees
    • Self-employed individuals pay PT as per state-defined frequency
    • Some states have different slabs for salaried vs self-employed
    • PT is deductible under Section 16(iii) of the Income Tax Act

    States with Highest PT

    • Maharashtra: Maximum ₹2,500/year (₹300/month for salary above ₹25,000)
    • Karnataka: Maximum ₹2,500/year (₹200/month for salary ₹15,001–₹25,000)
    • Tamil Nadu: Maximum ₹2,500/year based on half-yearly slabs
    • West Bengal: Maximum ₹2,500/year with quarterly slab structure
    • Telangana: Maximum ₹2,500/year following similar slabs as Andhra Pradesh

    Common Mistakes to Avoid

    ⚠️

    Confusing PT with Income Tax

    Professional Tax is a separate state-level tax deducted from salary. It is not part of income tax. PT is deductible under Section 16(iii) from your salary income before computing income tax.

    ⚠️

    Using Wrong State Slabs

    PT slabs vary significantly between states. Using another state's slab structure will give incorrect results. Always use the slabs of the state where you are employed or registered.

    ⚠️

    Ignoring the ₹2,500 Annual Cap

    The Constitution caps PT at ₹2,500 per year. Even if monthly slabs suggest a higher annual amount, the actual PT cannot exceed this limit. Always check the annual cap.

    ⚠️

    Not Claiming PT as Income Tax Deduction

    Many taxpayers forget to claim the PT paid during the year as a deduction under Section 16(iii). This reduces your taxable salary income. Ensure it reflects in your Form 16 and ITR.

    ⚠️

    Assuming All States Levy PT

    Not all Indian states levy Professional Tax. States like Delhi, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan, and Uttar Pradesh do not have PT. If you work in these states, your PT liability is zero.

    ⚠️

    Using CTC Instead of Gross Salary

    PT is calculated on gross monthly salary, not CTC. Using CTC (which includes variable pay, bonuses, etc.) may place you in a wrong slab and overstate your PT liability.

    Professional Tax Rules & Updates (2026-27)

    FY 2026-27 | Latest Updates
    • Constitutional Cap of ₹2,500: Under Article 276(2), the maximum Professional Tax any state can levy is ₹2,500 per annum per individual. This limit has not changed since the amendment.
    • PT Deduction under Income Tax: Professional Tax paid is deductible under Section 16(iii) of the Income Tax Act. This deduction is available under both old and new tax regimes, reducing your taxable salary income.
    • Employer Responsibility: Employers must deduct PT from employees' salaries, maintain registers, and deposit with the state government within due dates. Late payment attracts interest and penalties.
    • Self-Employed Registration: Self-employed individuals in PT-levying states must obtain a Professional Tax Registration Certificate (PTRC) and pay PT directly to the state. Payment frequency varies by state.
    • E-Payment Availability: Most states now offer online PT payment through their commercial tax portals, making it easier to comply without visiting government offices.
    • States Without PT: Delhi, Haryana, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh, and Nagaland do not levy Professional Tax. Residents saving PT by working in these states.
    • Penalty for Non-Compliance: Late payment of PT attracts interest (1-2% per month depending on state) and penalties. Employers failing to deduct PT may be liable to pay it themselves.
    • PT Slab Changes: States periodically revise PT slabs. Always verify with your state's latest notification. This calculator uses the most recently available slab data.

    Understanding Professional Tax in India

    Professional Tax is a state-level tax levied on individuals who earn income through employment, profession, trade, or business. It is authorized under Article 276 of the Constitution of India and is collected by state governments to fund local civic amenities and development projects.

    Who Needs to Pay Professional Tax?

    All individuals earning income in states that levy PT are liable to pay it. This includes salaried employees in government and private sectors, professionals like doctors, lawyers, chartered accountants, and consultants, proprietors and partners of firms, and self-employed individuals and freelancers.

    How is PT Different from Income Tax?

    • Governing Authority: PT is levied by state governments; Income Tax is levied by the Central Government
    • Basis: PT is based on gross salary; Income Tax is based on total taxable income after deductions
    • Maximum Amount: PT is capped at ₹2,500/year; Income Tax has no upper limit
    • Slab Structure: PT slabs are set by each state; Income Tax slabs are uniform across India
    • Deduction: PT paid is deductible from salary income under Section 16(iii) of the Income Tax Act

    PT for Self-Employed Individuals

    Self-employed individuals must register with the state's commercial tax department and obtain a Professional Tax Registration Certificate (PTRC). Payment frequency varies: monthly in some states, quarterly or half-yearly in others. The PT amount for self-employed is often a fixed amount based on gross receipts or turnover, rather than slab-based like salaried employees.

    Exemptions and Reliefs

    Many states provide exemptions for specific categories. Armed forces personnel are generally exempt. Parents or guardians of mentally or physically disabled persons may be exempt in certain states. Individuals above 65 years of age may qualify for exemption in some states. Women may receive exemptions or concessions in select states. Always verify with your state's latest PT notification.
