What Is a CTC Calculator?

A CTC (Cost to Company) calculator is an essential tool for salaried professionals in India to understand the true composition of their salary. CTC represents the total expenditure an employer incurs on an employee — it is not the same as the amount you take home every month. The gap between CTC and your in-hand salary can be as high as 30-35%, depending on components like employer PF, gratuity, insurance, and other non-cash benefits.

This calculator helps you convert between Gross Salary and CTC in both directions. If you know your gross salary and want to understand the total cost your employer bears, enter the gross amount. If you have a CTC figure from your offer letter and want to know your gross salary, switch to the CTC-to-Gross mode. The tool provides a complete breakdown of every component — Basic, HRA, Special Allowance, Employer PF, Gratuity, and Other Benefits — so you can see exactly where your money goes.

Whether you are evaluating a job offer, negotiating your compensation package, or simply trying to decode your salary slip, this calculator gives you the clarity you need. Understanding CTC vs Gross Salary vs Take-Home Salary is fundamental to financial planning, and this tool makes the calculation instant and transparent.

Understanding the Salary Components

  • Basic Salary: The core component of your salary, usually 40-50% of Gross. All statutory calculations (PF, gratuity, ESI) are based on this figure.
  • House Rent Allowance (HRA): Typically 20-30% of Gross. Can be partially or fully exempt from tax under the old tax regime if you pay rent.
  • Special Allowance: The balancing component that makes up the remaining percentage of Gross Salary. It is fully taxable.
  • Employer PF Contribution: 12% of Basic Salary contributed by the employer to your EPF account. Part of CTC but not part of your take-home.
  • Gratuity: 4.81% of Basic (equivalent to 15/26 of one month's basic per year of service). Accrued in CTC but payable only after 5 years of service.

Formula Used

The relationship between Gross Salary and CTC involves understanding which components are included in each. Gross Salary consists of direct earnings, while CTC adds the employer's obligations on top.

Formulas
Gross Salary = Basic + HRA + Special Allowance Basic = Basic% × Gross Salary Employer PF = Employer PF% × Basic Gratuity = Gratuity% × Basic CTC = Gross Salary + Employer PF + Gratuity + Other Benefits

Reverse Calculation: CTC to Gross

When converting from CTC to Gross Salary, the calculator works backwards:

Reverse Formula
Gross = (CTC − Other Benefits) ÷ (1 + Employer PF% × Basic% + Gratuity% × Basic%)

This formula accounts for the fact that Employer PF and Gratuity are percentages of Basic, which itself is a percentage of Gross. By solving algebraically, we derive the Gross Salary from the CTC figure.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

Many professionals misunderstand the relationship between CTC, Gross Salary, and Take-Home Salary. Avoid these common pitfalls when evaluating your compensation.

✕

Assuming CTC Equals Take-Home Salary

CTC includes employer PF, gratuity, insurance, and other non-cash benefits that never reach your bank account. Your actual take-home can be 30-35% lower than CTC. Always calculate the in-hand component before accepting an offer.

✕

Comparing Only CTC Across Offers

Two offers with identical CTC can have vastly different take-home salaries depending on the salary structure. A higher basic percentage means more PF deduction but also more gratuity and retirement savings. Compare gross and in-hand, not just CTC.

✕

Ignoring Gratuity as a Component

Gratuity at 4.81% of Basic is a significant non-cash addition to CTC. You only receive it after 5 years of service. If you switch jobs frequently, this component has uncertain value and should not be weighted heavily in comparisons.

✕

Not Verifying Basic Salary Percentage

A low basic salary (below 40%) reduces your PF corpus and gratuity, affecting long-term retirement savings. A very high basic reduces monthly take-home. Ensure the basic percentage is reasonable — typically 40-50% of gross.

✕

Confusing Employer PF with Employee PF

Employer PF (12% of Basic) is part of CTC but goes to your EPF account. Employee PF (also 12% of Basic) is deducted from your gross salary. Both are separate — employer PF inflates CTC, while employee PF reduces take-home.

✕

Forgetting Percentages Should Sum to 100%

Basic, HRA, and Special Allowance percentages must add up to 100% of Gross Salary. If they don't, the salary structure is incorrect and the CTC calculation will be inaccurate.

Latest Government Rules — FY 2025-26

Stay updated with the most recent statutory rules that affect CTC calculation and salary structuring for salaried employees in India.

FY 2025-26 / AY 2026-27

Key Rules Affecting CTC Computation

  • Provident Fund: Employee and Employer PF contributions remain at 12% of Basic + DA. The wage ceiling for mandatory PF enrolment is ₹15,000/month basic. Of the employer's 12%, 8.33% goes to EPS (capped at ₹15,000 wages = ₹1,250/month) and 3.67% to EPF.
  • Gratuity: Calculated at 4.81% of Basic (15/26 × Basic per year of service). The Payment of Gratuity Act applies to establishments with 10+ employees. Maximum tax-free gratuity is ₹30 lakh under Section 10(10) of the Income Tax Act for non-government employees covered under the Act.
  • ESI: Applicable for employees drawing gross wages up to ₹21,000/month. Employee contribution is 0.75% and employer contribution is 3.25%. Employer ESI contribution is part of CTC.
  • Professional Tax: Continues to vary by state. Maximum ₹2,500/year. PT is deductible from gross salary, not an addition to CTC.
  • New Tax Regime: Standard deduction increased to ₹75,000. The new regime is the default option. Employer NPS contribution deduction limit is 14% of basic for private employees under the old regime.
  • Labour Code Changes: The proposed Labour Codes may redefine "wages" to cap allowances at 50% of total compensation, potentially increasing basic salary and consequently PF and gratuity contributions across organizations. Monitor implementation status.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about CTC, Gross Salary, and salary components

What is CTC?
CTC stands for Cost to Company. It is the total amount an employer spends on an employee in a year, including gross salary, employer PF contribution, gratuity, insurance premiums, and any other benefits or perks. CTC is always higher than the take-home salary because it includes components that are not directly paid to the employee.
What is the difference between CTC and Gross Salary?
CTC (Cost to Company) is the total expenditure the employer incurs on an employee, including employer PF, gratuity, insurance, and other benefits. Gross Salary is what the employee earns before statutory deductions like PF, PT, and TDS. The key difference is that CTC includes employer contributions that are not part of gross salary. Gross Salary = CTC − Employer PF − Gratuity − Other Employer Benefits.
What is the difference between CTC and Take Home Salary?
CTC is the total cost the employer bears, while Take Home Salary is the actual amount credited to your bank account after all deductions. Take Home Salary = Gross Salary − Employee PF − Professional Tax − Income Tax (TDS) − Other Deductions. Typically, take-home salary is 65-80% of CTC depending on the salary structure.
Is CTC always higher than Gross Salary?
Yes, CTC is always higher than or equal to Gross Salary. CTC includes employer PF contribution, gratuity, insurance premiums, and other benefits on top of the gross salary. In the rare case where there are no employer contributions or benefits, CTC would equal Gross Salary, but this is practically never the case in organized employment.
What components are included in CTC?
CTC includes: Basic Salary, House Rent Allowance (HRA), Special Allowance, Dearness Allowance (DA), Leave Travel Allowance (LTA), Employer Provident Fund contribution (12% of basic), Gratuity (4.81% of basic), health insurance premiums, life insurance, meal coupons, transport allowance, communication allowance, and any other perks or benefits provided by the employer.
How is CTC calculated from Gross Salary?
CTC is calculated by adding all employer costs to the Gross Salary. CTC = Gross Salary + Employer PF Contribution + Gratuity + Insurance Premiums + Other Benefits. Employer PF is typically 12% of Basic Salary, and Gratuity is 4.81% of Basic Salary. For example, if Gross Salary is ₹10,00,000 with Basic at 40%, Employer PF (12% of ₹4,00,000) is ₹48,000 and Gratuity (4.81% of ₹4,00,000) is ₹19,240, making CTC = ₹10,67,240 plus any other benefits.
What is Employer's PF contribution in CTC?
Employer's PF contribution is 12% of Basic Salary (plus DA, if applicable). This amount is part of CTC but is not paid to the employee directly. Out of this 12%, 8.33% goes to the Employee Pension Scheme (EPS) capped at ₹15,000 wages, and the remaining 3.67% goes to the Employee Provident Fund (EPF). This is a retirement benefit that accrues in the employee's EPF account.
What is Gratuity in CTC?
Gratuity is a statutory retirement benefit under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972. It is calculated at 4.81% of Basic Salary (which equals 15/26 of one month's basic for each year of service). Gratuity is included in CTC as an accrual, but the employee receives it only upon leaving the company after completing at least 5 years of continuous service. The maximum tax-free gratuity limit is ₹30 lakh.
Can CTC be negotiated?
Yes, CTC can be negotiated during job offers and annual appraisals. While the total CTC figure is negotiable, you can also negotiate the salary structure — such as requesting a higher basic salary (which increases PF and gratuity but may reduce take-home) or higher variable pay (which may boost CTC on paper but is performance-dependent). Always evaluate the take-home component, not just the CTC figure.
Is higher CTC always better?
Not necessarily. A higher CTC may include large non-cash components like gratuity accrual, employer PF, or stock options that do not increase your monthly take-home pay. Two offers with the same CTC can yield very different in-hand salaries depending on the salary structure. Always compare the gross salary, take-home pay, and the composition of CTC rather than just the headline CTC number.
How is Gross Salary calculated from CTC?
Gross Salary is derived by subtracting employer-only costs from CTC. Gross Salary = CTC − Employer PF Contribution − Gratuity − Insurance Premiums − Other Employer Benefits. Since Employer PF is 12% of Basic and Gratuity is 4.81% of Basic, and Basic is typically 40% of Gross, you can reverse-calculate: Gross = (CTC − Other Benefits) ÷ (1 + 0.12 × Basic% + 0.0481 × Basic%).
What percentage of CTC is usually Basic Salary?
Basic Salary is typically 40-50% of CTC in most Indian companies. Some organizations set it at 30-35% to reduce statutory obligations like PF and gratuity, while others may go up to 50-60% for roles where retirement benefits are prioritized. A higher basic salary means higher PF contributions and gratuity, but lower take-home pay due to larger PF deductions.
