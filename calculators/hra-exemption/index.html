Understanding HRA Exemption

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is a common component of salary structure provided by employers to employees to meet the cost of rental accommodation. Under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act, a portion of HRA received is exempt from tax, subject to certain conditions. This exemption is available only to salaried individuals who actually pay rent for their accommodation and opt for the old tax regime.

HRA exemption is one of the most significant tax-saving tools for salaried employees living in rented accommodation. It can substantially reduce your taxable income and help you save on income tax each financial year. The exemption amount is calculated as the minimum of three conditions prescribed under Rule 2A of the Income Tax Rules, ensuring a fair and standardized approach to determining the tax-free portion of your HRA.

Formula Used for HRA Exemption

The HRA exemption is the least (minimum) of the following three amounts calculated under Section 10(13A) read with Rule 2A:

HRA Exemption = MINIMUM of:

1 Actual HRA Received â€” The total HRA received from your employer during the financial year.
2 50% of (Basic + DA) for Metro Cities or 40% of (Basic + DA) for Non-Metro Cities â€” Metro cities are Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai. All other cities fall under non-metro.
3 Rent Paid âˆ’ 10% of (Basic + DA) â€” The excess of annual rent paid over 10% of your basic salary plus dearness allowance.

The smallest of these three values is your HRA exemption â€” the tax-free portion. The remaining HRA (actual HRA received minus exemption) gets added to your taxable salary income.

Conditions for Claiming HRA Exemption

To claim HRA exemption, you must meet all of the following conditions:

  • You must receive HRA: HRA should be a component of your salary structure provided by your employer. Self-employed individuals cannot claim HRA exemption.
  • You must actually pay rent: You should be living in a rented accommodation and actually paying rent. You cannot claim HRA if you live in your own house.
  • Old Tax Regime: HRA exemption is available only under the old tax regime. Under the new tax regime, the entire HRA received is fully taxable.
  • Proper documentation: You must maintain valid rent receipts and rent agreement. If rent exceeds â‚¹1,00,000 per year, landlord's PAN is mandatory.

Documents Required for HRA Exemption

To claim HRA exemption and be prepared for any income tax scrutiny, maintain these documents:

  • Rent Agreement: A valid rent agreement on stamp paper, preferably registered, between you and your landlord.
  • Rent Receipts: Monthly or quarterly rent receipts from your landlord with their signature, address, and PAN (if rent exceeds â‚¹1 lakh/year).
  • PAN of Landlord: Mandatory if annual rent exceeds â‚¹1,00,000. Required to be submitted to your employer for TDS calculation.
  • Bank Statements: Proof of rent payment through bank transfer, NEFT, RTGS, or UPI is strongly recommended over cash payments.

Special Cases

There are some special scenarios regarding HRA exemption that taxpayers should be aware of:

  • Living with Parents: If you pay genuine rent to your parents, you can claim HRA exemption. Your parents must declare this rent as income in their tax returns.
  • Own House in Different City: If you own a house in one city but work and live on rent in another city, you can claim HRA exemption for the rent paid in the other city.
  • Home Loan and HRA: You can claim both home loan interest deduction under Section 24 and HRA exemption simultaneously if you own a house (with home loan) in one city but pay rent in another city where you work.
  • Change of Employment: If you change jobs mid-year, aggregate HRA received from both employers and total rent paid for the entire year to calculate the exemption.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

These frequent errors can lead to rejected claims, penalties, or income tax notices

âš 

Claiming HRA Under New Tax Regime

HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is NOT available under the new tax regime. If you have opted for the new regime, the entire HRA is fully taxable. Always verify your regime choice before claiming HRA.

âš 

Paying Rent to Spouse Without Genuine Proof

Paying rent to your spouse is highly scrutinized by the Income Tax Department. Since spouses are presumed to jointly own household assets, such arrangements are frequently disallowed. Ensure genuine documentation and that the spouse declares it as income.

âš 

Not Providing Landlord PAN for Rent Above â‚¹1 Lakh

If your annual rent exceeds â‚¹1,00,000, providing your landlord's PAN to your employer is mandatory. Failure to do so can result in your HRA claim being disallowed or higher TDS deduction. Collect PAN from your landlord well in advance.

âš 

Using Gross Salary Instead of Basic + DA

A common error is using gross salary or CTC for HRA calculation. HRA exemption is calculated only on Basic Salary + Dearness Allowance (DA). Other components like HRA itself, special allowances, bonuses, and commissions (unless they form part of retirement benefits) are excluded.

âš 

Including Maintenance Charges in Rent

Only the actual rent component is considered for HRA calculation. Society maintenance charges, electricity, water, gas, and other utility bills paid to the landlord or society are NOT part of rent paid. Including them inflates your claim incorrectly.

âš 

Not Keeping Rent Receipts for the Full Year

Many employees forget to collect rent receipts for every month. In case of a tax notice, you must produce receipts for the entire financial year. Maintain monthly receipts with landlord's signature, date, amount, and address. Digital payments should have corresponding receipts.

Stay updated with the current rules governing HRA exemption for the assessment year 2026-27

Important

HRA NOT Available Under New Tax Regime

From FY 2020-21 onwards, HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is completely removed under the new tax regime. As per the latest rules for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27), if you choose the new tax regime, the entire HRA received from your employer is added to your taxable salary. You must opt for the old tax regime to claim any HRA benefit.

Mandatory

Landlord PAN Required If Annual Rent Exceeds â‚¹1,00,000

Under Rule 26A of the Income Tax Rules, if the total rent paid by you in a financial year exceeds â‚¹1,00,000 (â‚¹8,333 per month), you must provide your landlord's PAN to your employer. This is required for your employer to compute TDS correctly. If the landlord does not have a PAN, a declaration along with the landlord's Aadhaar number, Form 60, must be submitted.

TDS Rule

TDS on Rent Exceeding â‚¹2,40,000 Per Year

Under Section 194-IB, if you pay rent exceeding â‚¹2,40,000 per year (â‚¹50,000 per month for non-residential or â‚¹2,00,000 per month threshold for residential), your landlord or you (as tenant in certain cases) may be liable for TDS. For individual tenants paying rent for residential property exceeding â‚¹50,000 per month, TDS at 5% must be deducted under Section 194-IB.

FY 2025-26

Old Regime Remains Default Option for Salaried with HRA

Since Budget 2023, the new tax regime is the default regime. However, salaried employees who wish to claim HRA exemption must explicitly opt for the old tax regime. Your employer will ask you to choose the regime at the beginning of each financial year. If you do not choose, the new regime applies by default and your HRA becomes fully taxable.

Frequently Asked Questions

Everything you need to know about HRA exemption, tax rules, and claims

What is HRA exemption?
HRA (House Rent Allowance) exemption is a tax benefit under Section 10(13A) of the Income Tax Act available to salaried employees who receive HRA as part of their salary and pay rent for accommodation. A portion of HRA received is exempt from tax based on the minimum of three conditions â€” actual HRA received, a percentage of basic salary, and rent paid minus 10% of basic salary.
How is HRA exemption calculated?
HRA exemption is the least of three amounts: (1) Actual HRA received from employer, (2) 50% of Basic+DA for metro cities or 40% for non-metro cities, (3) Rent paid minus 10% of Basic+DA. The lowest value becomes the exempt amount. The remaining HRA is added to your taxable income. The exemption is available only under the old tax regime.
Which cities are considered metro for HRA exemption?
Only four cities are considered metro for HRA purposes: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, and Chennai. If you reside in any of these cities, you get the benefit of 50% of Basic+DA as the maximum limit. For all other cities, including Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, and Ahmedabad, the limit is 40% of Basic+DA. These are the only cities notified by the Income Tax Department as metros for HRA calculation.
Is HRA exemption available under the new tax regime?
No. HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is NOT available under the new tax regime. If you opt for the new tax regime, the entire HRA received is taxable as part of your salary income. This applies from FY 2020-21 onwards. To claim HRA, you must explicitly choose the old tax regime with higher rates but more deductions and exemptions.
Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents?
Yes, you can claim HRA exemption if you pay genuine rent to your parents. However, the arrangement must be real and supported by proper documentation â€” rent agreement, rent receipts, and bank transfer proof. Your parents must declare this rent as income in their tax returns. The Income Tax Department may scrutinize such arrangements to ensure they are not sham transactions.
Can I claim HRA and home loan deduction together?
Yes, you can claim both HRA exemption and home loan interest deduction under Section 24 simultaneously. This is possible if you own a house (with a home loan) in one city but are required to live on rent in another city due to your job. Both benefits are available only under the old tax regime. The home loan property should not be self-occupied in the city where you are claiming HRA.
What if my annual rent exceeds â‚¹1,00,000?
If your annual rent exceeds â‚¹1,00,000 (i.e., more than â‚¹8,333 per month), you must provide your landlord's PAN to your employer. This is mandatory under Rule 26A. If your landlord does not have a PAN, they need to submit a declaration with their Aadhaar number using Form 60. Without the landlord's PAN, your HRA claim for the excess amount may be disallowed.
Do I need rent receipts to claim HRA exemption?
Yes, rent receipts are essential for claiming HRA exemption. You should collect monthly or quarterly rent receipts from your landlord. Each receipt should include the landlord's name, address, PAN (if rent exceeds â‚¹1 lakh/year), the period, amount paid, and signature. While a registered rent agreement is recommended, receipts are the primary document required as proof of rent payment.
Can I claim HRA if I live in my own house?
No, you cannot claim HRA exemption if you live in your own house. HRA exemption is available only if you actually pay rent for accommodation you do not own. However, if you own a house in one city and live on rent in another city due to work, you can claim HRA exemption for the rent paid in the other city.
Is HRA exemption available to self-employed individuals?
No. HRA exemption under Section 10(13A) is available only to salaried individuals who receive HRA as a component of their salary structure from an employer. Self-employed persons, freelancers, and business owners cannot claim HRA exemption. However, self-employed individuals can claim rent paid as a business expense if the rented premises are used for business purposes.
What is the minimum rent I must pay to get any HRA benefit?
There is no fixed minimum rent amount, but effectively, your annual rent must exceed 10% of your Basic+DA for Condition 3 to produce a positive value. If your rent is less than or equal to 10% of Basic+DA, Condition 3 becomes zero or negative, and since it's likely the minimum, your HRA exemption becomes nil. So your rent must be more than 10% of Basic+DA to claim any meaningful exemption.
Can I claim HRA without a registered rent agreement?
A registered rent agreement is recommended but not strictly mandatory for claiming HRA exemption. The primary documents required are rent receipts. However, having a registered agreement strengthens your claim significantly in case of a tax scrutiny. Some states require mandatory registration for rent agreements exceeding 11 months, so check your local laws.
Can I claim HRA exemption for rent paid in cash?
While there is no explicit prohibition on paying rent in cash for HRA claims, it is strongly discouraged. The Income Tax Department may question large cash transactions. It is always advisable to pay rent through bank transfer (NEFT, RTGS, UPI, or cheque) to maintain a clear audit trail. Cash payments without proper receipts may lead to your claim being disallowed during scrutiny.
